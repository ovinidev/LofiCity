# LoFi City: developer guide

The full reference that used to live in the README: every city and scene, behaviour rules, URL options, keys, code layout and detailed credits. The short pt-BR overview is in [README.md](../README.md); agent rules are in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

Lo-fi pixel-art scenes that follow each city's **real clock** and **live weather**. If it's raining there, it rains on screen; if it's night there, it's night here. Comes with two radio stations playing real music released under Creative Commons.

**Live:** https://loficity-lac.vercel.app

**Cities** (pick one on the map: tap the city name in the panel or press T; Esperança is the default)

- **Campina Grande**: fireworks most nights of its famous São João in June.
  - `Açude Velho`: the glass drums of the Museu de Arte Popular (the "Três Pandeiros") and the high-rises mirrored in the water.
  - `Pirâmide do Parque do Povo` (`piramide-parque-do-povo`): the salmon concrete A-frame across an empty paved lot, with floodlight masts, a borracharia and the city's towers behind. Most of the year it stands quiet with its floodlights off; in June it fills up for the São João: bandeirolas, stalls and dancers under the roof, a fogueira, the floodlights on and "O MAIOR SÃO JOÃO DO MUNDO" in neon along its legs.
  - `Estação Velha`: the yellow 1907 Great Western station, now the Museu do Algodão: terracotta roofs, white quoins, "GWBR" on the gable and the "CAMPINA GRANDE" plaque, the iron railing, a castanhola and the old steam locomotive under its pavilion. Doors open on lit rooms while the museum is open.
  - `Parque da Criança`: a wide view across the park: the cycle and walking path, the sand field with a kickabout by day, royal and coconut palms, the "EU ♥ CORRER" letters, a cobbled plaza, the skate bowl and the old brick chimney, Catolé's towers beyond. No traffic; busiest with runners at dawn and late afternoon, nearly empty late at night.
  - `Partage Shopping`: the mall on Av. Prefeito Severino Cabral: store boards, "ESTACIONAMENTO" over the parking entrance, the red flower emblem and "PARTAGE SHOPPING", the long salmon louvred façade, palms and a bus stop on the busy avenue. Signs and glass light up after dark while it is open. Its hours are shared with the inside (`src/scenes/partage-rules.ts`, art direction, not the mall's real timetable): Monday to Saturday 10h-22h, Sundays 14h-20h. Tapping the main doors under the glass marquee goes inside while it is open; to make the way in easy to spot, a red `ENTRADA` board hangs on the marquee (lit at night while open, with a small pulsing arrow down to the doors), the two door bays have steel frames and a mat, and at night the doors spill warm light onto the forecourt (art direction: the photo shows the marquee, not a sign).
  - `Partage · Lojas` (`partage-interior`): the shops inside that mall, a two-storey gallery seen side-on across the corridor. A long glass skylight (the city's real sky shows through it; drops run down the panes only with real rain or the labelled `#wx=` test), the upper row of shop fronts behind the walkway's glass balustrade, the lower row on the ground floor, an escalator climbing between them with people riding it, the glass doors back out to the forecourt under a green `SAÍDA` sign, a coffee kiosk, planters and a bench on the polished floor. Three fronts follow reference photos of the mall's shops supplied by the owner in issue #40 (not committed), under made-up look-alike names, never the real brands: a department store with a dark band and backlit white letters (`RIACHINHO`: racks, mannequins, one in a red dress, a display table), a bookshop with a wood fascia (`LEITORA`: shelves and low tables of books) and a sports store with red letters on white and a red `SALDOS` board (`CENTAURA`). The other shops show only a category (`MODA`, `BRINQUEDOS`, ...); the gallery's layout itself is a generic Brazilian mall, not photographed. On the upper floor, between the bookshop and the perfumery, a passage signposted `ALIMENTAÇÃO / CINEMA` leads to the food court. Indoors, so it lights itself the same by day and night (a touch warmer at night) and the weather does not reach the people walking the corridors (shared pedestrians with `indoor: true`: no umbrellas, no hurrying, no heat drinks). It follows the street front's hours: the crowd grows in the evening and at weekends and thins out over the last 15 minutes, while the shops pull their roll-down grilles one after another; closed (only reachable with `#scene=`), the grilles are down, the lights are dimmed to the security level, the escalator stands still and nobody is inside. Tapping the kiosk's attendant gets a coffee-counter line, tapping the passage goes to the food court, and tapping `SAÍDA` goes back out.
  - `Partage · Praça de alimentação` (`partage-alimentacao`): the mall's food court with the cinema's front at its end, seen side-on, after the food-court and cinema photos in issue #40 (not committed). A tall white hall with three skylight boxes and a band of clerestory glass (the real sky shows through; rain on the panes only with a real reading), two white columns branching in a Y up to the roof, a row of fast-food counters with lit boards under a grey band of round speakers (they show only what they sell: `SUCOS`, `TAPIOCA`, `LANCHÃO`, `BURGER`, `PASTEL`), black tables with yellow chairs on a black-and-white floor and low planters of grass. Then the cinema, `CINESERTÃO CINEMAS` in big red letters on a beige wall (a made-up name after the photo's chain): the grey `BILHETERIA` band, the dark red counter wall with three ticket windows and posters (generic colour blocks), the `SALAS` entrance, a screen playing trailer-like colour shots and a queue between stanchions while tickets are on sale. The counters keep the mall's hours (art direction): staff at work while open, grilles coming down one by one before closing. Diners at the tables (seeded per seat, a new set every few minutes, reproducible with `#at=`) and the queue grow with the mall's crowd (`insideCrowd`). Indoors, lit by the mall like the shops (`mallGrade()` in `src/scenes/partage-kit.ts`). Tapping someone at a counter gets a counter line, tapping a diner a food-court line, tapping `LOJAS` goes back to the shops and tapping the box office goes into the cinema while it is open (otherwise a bubble says when it opens).
  - `Cinema · Partage` (`cinema-partage`): the cinema inside Partage Shopping. It is the same room as `Cinema · Remígio` (the shared `cinemaRoom()` in `src/scenes/cinema-remigio.ts`: screen, seats, the code-drawn generic action film, the room lit by the picture) with the same art-direction sessions (`src/scenes/cinema-rules.ts`, 14:00-23:00) but its own seeded film order (a salt on `shotAt`), and its `SAÍDA` leads back to the food court. Its doors (the box office in `Partage · Praça de alimentação`) open while both the mall and the cinema are open (`cinemaDoorTap` in `src/scenes/partage-rules.ts`); otherwise a bubble says "Fechado, volta às 14h" (or the mall's own note). The last session runs past the mall's closing time, so leaving late lands in the closed food court. No reference photo of this cinema was used. Tapping someone in the audience gets a whispered movie line.
  - `Terminal de Integração`: the long bus terminal on R. Dom Pedro II: the "SITRANS / STTP" and "TARIFA ZERO" board, concrete fins (one with a painted mural), green railings and passengers on the benches. City buses pull into the bays, wait and leave, more often at rush hour, none in the small hours. Tapping a bus boards it (see `Ônibus`).

  - `Ônibus` (`onibus`): riding a city bus, seen from an aisle seat across to the far wall. The side windows show the city scrolling past (in Campina Grande high-rises behind houses), and inside there are the seats along the wall, the poles and handrail with straps, the turnstile at the front and the passengers. They sit along the wall and, at the rush hours, also stand in the aisle, some looking out of the window and some facing the viewer. Tapping a passenger gets a bus line in a speech bubble. The bus also opens from any Paraíba street scene: tapping a moving bus boards that bus, with its own interior. São José buses have green seats and chrome poles, the micro-ônibus has three windows, red seats and a rear door, and the prefeitura school bus has brown bench seats, no turnstile and children in uniform. The STTP buses at the Terminal de Integração have blue seats, yellow poles and a red turnstile. The lit green `SAÍDA` sign on the ceiling (the same sign as in the mall and the cinema rooms), or Esc, goes back to the scene you boarded from. Each city has its own street outside: Esperança and Remígio have market streets with shops, stalls and the church on the hills, João Pessoa has the sea, sand, kiosks and palms, and Areia has colonial two-storey houses with green hills behind. The sky in the windows is the city's real sky. Rain from a real reading runs down the glass and mists the lower panes. At night street lamps and lit windows pass by and the cabin lamps glow warm. Art direction, not real timetables (`src/scenes/onibus-rules.ts`): STTP runs 04:30-23:30, São José and the micro 05:00-23:00, and the school bus runs on weekdays around the school shifts (06:00-07:48, 11:00-13:30, 17:00-18:30). Outside those hours the bus is parked in the garage with dimmed lights and nobody on board. How full it is follows a commuter curve (packed at 06-08h and 17-19h, quiet at midday, nearly empty late, lighter at weekends). It drives and stops on a fixed 47 s cycle, and people get on and off only while it stands at a stop. Drawn from Creative Commons photos of Brazilian city-bus interiors on Wikimedia Commons, as reference only (Curitiba, by Morio, CC BY-SA 3.0; Rio de Janeiro, by Eduardo P, CC BY-SA 3.0; a Caio bus from Flickr, CC BY 2.0) and Campina Grande street photos (CC BY 2.0 / CC BY-SA 3.0). None are in the repo, and the street outside is generic for each city.

  **Doors**: tapping the door of `Cine · Fachada` goes inside (`Cinema · Remígio`) at the same moment: tapping the lit `SAÍDA` sign at the foot of the aisle goes back out to the street front. While the cinema is closed the door does not open: a bubble says "Fechado, volta às 14h". A door is an optional `goto` (scene id of the same city) on a scene's tap result, handled in `main.ts` by the same scene change as the scene button (bubbles and people cleared, the scene rebuilt at the same time, `#scene=` in the address bar updated); the doors and exits get the floating arrow of the tap hints (below). Both scenes stay in the scene cycle (S and the scene button) and in the random pick on load, like any other scene. Partage Shopping in Campina works the same way: its main doors lead to `Partage · Por dentro` while the mall is open (closed: "Fechado, abre às 10h", or "abre amanhã às ..." after closing), and the `SAÍDA` over the glass doors inside leads back out; inside, the shops and the food court lead to each other (the `ALIMENTAÇÃO / CINEMA` passage and `LOJAS`), and the food court's box office leads into the cinema.

  **Tap hints**: in the first seconds after a scene opens (about 1 to 9 s, faded in and out; `src/fx/hints-rules.ts`, drawn by `src/fx/hints.ts`), doors that work and buses you can board get a small animated icon: a bouncing white arrow over a door, a small bus with a blinking tick over a bus. They stay off once you have used a door or boarded a bus, while a dialog is open, and for shut doors. A scene lists its doors / bays through the optional `hintSpots()`; street buses come from the traffic pool. Everything is drawn in code and driven by the clock.

- **Esperança, Paraíba, Brazil** (Brasília time). Local calendar: the patron saint's festival in January, Holy Week, São João in June, Christmas.
  - `Praça da Matriz`: the yellow Nossa Senhora do Bom Conselho church with its slender clock tower and spire, seen from the street: the paved praça with trees and lamps on the left, and on the right the road toward Campina Grande behind a yellow curb, with shops, poles and wires across it. Traffic only uses that road, drawn in perspective (going away in the right lane, oncoming in the left), and stops for people on the zebra crossing.
  - `Rua do Comércio`: the main shopping street, with a bar that puts its tables out at night.
  - `Capelinha das Pedras`: the tiny 1925 chapel on the Lajedo do Araçá rock, beside the pool that mirrors it, painted the Matriz's yellow. A quiet spot: no traffic and usually nobody around. Now and then a visitor walks up to take photos, and on some days (more often on Sundays, always on 27 June and 1 January) there is one short daytime mass: the door opens on a lit interior, a priest waits at the door and a handful of people come and go. This is art direction, not the chapel's real schedule: each day's events come from a seed of the local date (so `#at=` shows the same day), all between 07:00 and 17:00, and the weather does not change them.
  - `Praça da Cultura`: the town's postcard square: broad blue steps up to a red plaza, red steps up to the cream-yellow building with its outlined arched blue door, royal palms and an araucária, the red and white "EU ♥ ESPERANÇA" letters with their big heart, craft fairs and the Paixão de Cristo in Holy Week. The school is the large cream-yellow building at the back of the square. On weekdays students in the public-school uniform (white tee, blue trousers, backpack) climb the blue and red steps to it from 06:30 and 12:30 and vanish through its arched central entrance, and come out of it after 12:00 and 17:30; the blue door stands open from the first arrivals (06:30) to the last leavers (17:50), including the short lull between the shifts, and the classroom windows (the coloured casements of the wings and the small arched windows of the central bay) are open and lit only during the shifts (07:00-12:00 and 13:00-17:30); at weekends, at night and outside these hours the door is shut and the windows closed and dark. At weekends and at night the gate is shut and every window dark. After school and at weekends teenagers play a two- or three-a-side kick-about between pairs of backpacks on the left of the plaza (in a portrait frame, which does not reach the left lawn bed, on the lower paving right of the blue steps) and two-a-side volleyball over a portable net in front of the red steps (weekdays 14:00-17:30, Saturdays 08:00-11:30 and 14:00-17:30, Sundays 08:00-11:30 and 15:00-17:30), never at night, never during the craft fair or the Paixão, and only with a real weather reading: real rain pauses a game (the players wait at the back of the plaza) and none starts in it, unknown weather means nobody plays. School holidays and vacations are modelled as art direction too: no students, the door shut and the windows dark on the national holidays (1 Jan, 21 Apr, 1 May, 7 Sep, 12 Oct, 2 Nov, 15 Nov, 20 Nov, 25 Dec, plus Carnival Monday and Tuesday, Good Friday and Corpus Christi, which move with Easter), all of July (mid-year recess) and from 20 December to the end of January (long recess); the plaza games keep their own windows on those days. All these hours and dates are assumptions, not the school's real calendar; see `src/scenes/praca-cultura-schedule.ts` and `src/scenes/praca-games.ts`. Students and players can be tapped and talk about school, football or volleyball.
  - `Campo da Rodoviária` (`campo-rodoviaria`, the owner's "Campo do América"): the fenced artificial-turf pitch behind a wall of football murals, where two sides actually play: five outfield players and a keeper each, plus a referee, passing, dribbling, shooting and now and then scoring (a short celebration, then a kickoff), with spectators on the wall. A new match starts every 1h30 from 08:00 to 20:00 local time (08:00, 09:30, 11:00, 12:30, 14:00, 15:30, 17:00, 18:30, 20:00); each lasts until the next start and the last ends at 21:00, so there is no play before 08:00 or from 21:00. That duration is an artistic assumption, not a real fixture list. Each match gets a new pair of high-contrast kits (chosen from the local date and slot, so `#at=` shows the same kits) with keepers and referee in distinct colours; the 18:30 and 20:00 games are played under the lit floodlights. Heavy rain or a storm in the real reading pauses play (the players wait on the far touchline and the spectators leave); light rain plays on. The score is not shared between visitors: each viewer's match starts at 0-0 when they open the scene. Tapping a player shows a football line.
  - `Centro · Casa de Amigos` (`centro-teatro`): the frontage across the wide avenue through the centre: the tall cream-tiled block with dark glass bands and the "DECORAMA / CASA DE AMIGOS" billboard (lit by its floodlights at night), "ZARB", "JM BOLOS" and "ÓTICAS DINIZ" on the left, the white and red colonial front, "TUDO 10" and "LOJÃO" on the right, motorcycles at the far kerb and the traffic-light pole in the foreground. Esperança's streets are busy with local life: motorcycles of every kind (mototáxi, delivery, the gas man with a botijão), Viação São José buses, the prefeitura school bus, carros-pipa, donkey carts, the carro de som, pickups carrying furniture, a Veraneio now and then; joggers, sun umbrellas and street vendors; street dogs, cats on the walls, birds on the wires, vultures on the thermals and bats around the lamps.

Sun umbrellas and hot-day drinks follow the real temperature only (a live or stale Open-Meteo reading; never a made-up value). Sun umbrellas appear above 26 °C on a clear day (one rule, `parasolWeather` in `src/actors/rain-rules.ts`, for the side-on and the depth scenes). On hot clear days in the Paraíba cities (rules in `src/actors/heat-rules.ts`; thresholds are art-direction assumptions) some walkers carry a blue water bottle or a green coconut with a straw (a few px, so they read at this scale) and now and then stop for a couple of seconds to drink, raising the arm, tipping the drink to the mouth and tilting the head back: from 28 °C about 15% of new walkers, every 9–16 s; from 32 °C about 30%, every 5–10 s. Between 08:00 and 17:00 the street-food cart in Rua do Comércio and Praça da Matriz turns into a coconut-water cart (a pile of green coconuts, a vendor in a straw hat, an `AGUA DE COCO` cloth sign), and passers-by sometimes stop, buy one and drink next to it, one customer at a time. Praça da Cultura has no cart, so only the drinkers appear there; Tokyo keeps its own look. All of this needs daylight (the same limit as the sun umbrellas), a cloud cover under 50% and no real rain, and switches off entirely with unknown temperature or weather. The `#wx=` test override does not set a temperature, so it never triggers it; to preview it, stub the Open-Meteo response (e.g. a Playwright route) with a hot reading.

Everything is drawn in code, with no image assets: an indexed framebuffer whose palette is graded for day, night, sunset and overcast skies.

**Tap anything that moves**: birds fly off the wires, cats bolt along the walls, dogs bark and run, people answer with a short speech bubble, and every vehicle has its own horn, from the moto's beep to the São José bus, the donkey cart's bray and the carro de som's jingle. In Praça da Matriz, tap the church and its bell strikes once. All sounds are synthesized in the browser.

The bubbles are silent and come from an authored pool of gentle pt-BR lines with a Paraíba accent (simple pt-BR with a few Japanese words in Tokyo), picked for the city, the scene, the time of day (manhã, tarde, noite, madrugada) and the real weather only: heat lines (including the hot-day ones such as "Haja calor!" and "Bora tomar uma água de coco, visse?", the coconut ones by day only) need a real reading of 30 °C or more, cold lines a real low temperature, rain lines real precipitation (or the `#wx=` test), and the Esperança wind lines ("Que ventania!", "Segura o chapéu!", a player's "Esse vento leva a bola!", a feira vendor's "Segura a lona…") strong wind of 30 km/h or more from a reading (or the `#wind=` / `#wx=` tests); with no reading there are no weather lines at all. The same line doesn't come back right away, at most two bubbles show at once, each hides after about 3 s, and changing scene or city clears them. In the Capelinha only its visitors, the priest and the congregation speak.

**Little moments**: now and then (a few times an hour at most, one at a time) something small happens in Rua do Comércio, Praça da Cultura, Estação Velha, Parque da Criança and the Cabo Branco seafront: a street dog tags along behind someone, a picolé, pipoca or água de coco seller stops for a while, a kid kicks a ball on the sidewalk, a cat crosses a wall, someone feeds the pigeons, a delivery moto pulls over at a shop. They follow the city's clock (only the occasional dog at madrugada) and the real weather: no vendor, ball or pigeons in real rain or with no reading. The schedule is fixed by scene and time, so a `#at=` moment always shows the same thing (`#debug` shows the running and next one). With ambient sound on, the seller's little bell or whistle sounds once, softly, and never while the radio plays.

**Church bells (Esperança)**: with ambient sound on (M), the matriz strikes the hours on Esperança's real clock (Brasília time), from 05:00 through 00:00, counting in 12-hour style (13h = 1 strike, 00h = 12); it is silent from 01:00 to 04:00. You hear it in every Esperança scene: near in Praça da Matriz, farther away and softer elsewhere. Missed hours are never replayed (background tab, sleep, reload, turning sound on late), and nothing rings while a `#at=` moment is pinned.

**Daily rhythm**: crowds, traffic and birds follow each city's own clock, interpolated smoothly between hours (and `#at=` moments). Esperança, Campina Grande and João Pessoa all use Brasília time (UTC-3, the same as Fortaleza, no daylight saving), and so do the weekday and calendar.

- Esperança's Praça da Matriz and Praça da Cultura are busiest from 14h to 15h, and every city is livelier through the day than at night. Car traffic in the Paraíba towns builds up as the day goes on and peaks at 17–18h. At the shops (Armazém Paraíba most of all, Rua do Comércio, Centro · Casa de Amigos) vendors stand out front while the shop is open, and customers keep walking in and coming out with bags. Around 17h the birds get busy, flying back to the trees and wires (this depends on the time only, not the temperature). From 19h the streets thin out; 21–23h and 00–05h are nearly empty, with just the odd dog or passer-by.
- Campina Grande and João Pessoa are busier by day and in the evening, but still have few people out from 00h to 05h.
- **Mass at the Matriz**: mass is every day at 18h (Sundays also 07 and 09h): the doors open, the nave is lit and a small congregation gathers by the church. The doors also stand open every day from 09 to 12h and from 16 to 19h, when single people drop in to pray and come out again. Assumptions, not an official parish timetable: each mass lasts 1 hour. The rest of the praça stays quiet.
- Weather is never made up here: only real rain, storms and snow from Open-Meteo (live or stale) scale these crowd levels down; with no reading ("clima indisponível") the rhythm runs unscaled.

**Rain reactions**: in real rain or snow (rain as passed by the rain gate, see Credits; or the labelled `#wx=` test) most people open an umbrella and the rest hurry; with no reading nothing changes. The rules live in `src/actors/rain-rules.ts` and are the same in every scene: the side-on pedestrians, the depth streets (Rua Faustino Porto, Rua José Bezerra da Costa, Rua Manoel Rodrigues de Oliveira), the people crossing at the zebras of Centro · Casa de Amigos and Praça da Cultura, the students, the faithful at the Matriz, the cinema queue and the shop-front customers (Rua do Comércio, Centro · Casa de Amigos, Armazém Paraíba). On hot, clear afternoons (a real reading above 26 °C) some people carry a sun umbrella. People without an umbrella may wait out real rain under a drawn awning: the shop canopy at Centro · Casa de Amigos, the Partage entrance and bus shelter, and in Rua Manoel Rodrigues the pink corner building's canopy and the orange canopy of the shop on the right. Rua Faustino Porto and Rua José Bezerra da Costa have no awning in the drawing, so no shelter. Inside Partage Shopping nobody reacts to the weather at all (a scene passes `indoor: true` to `setLanes`). `test/scene-rules.test.ts` checks every registered scene against the shared rules (weather only from the feed, seeded randomness, rain reactions through `rain-rules.ts`, `rhythm.ts` densities, registration and docs, no image assets).

**Wind**: everything follows `S.wind`, built from the real Open-Meteo wind speed and direction in `src/sky/live.ts` as `clamp(km/h / 55, 0, 1.2) × sign × 0.9 + 0.05` (calm ≈ 0.05; positive blows west → east, i.e. to the right). `src/sky/wind-rules.ts` inverts it (`windKmh`), gives a 0–1 visual strength (`windStrength`, 1 from 45 km/h) and the direction (`windDir`), and calls wind **strong at 30 km/h or more**, i.e. `|S.wind − 0.05| ≥ 0.4909` (S.wind ≥ 0.541 one way, ≤ −0.441 the other); the `#wx=storm` preset (0.5, about 27 km/h) stays just below it. What reacts: street-tree canopies (`src/actors/trees.ts`; Praça da Matriz, Praça da Cultura, Rua Faustino Porto, Rua José Bezerra da Costa, and the other cities' trees too) lean and sway, the top more than the bottom, the trunk never; the coconut and royal palms (`drawPalm`; Praça da Matriz, Praça da Cultura, Campo da Rodoviária) sway faster and bend downwind with their fronds streaming; the festa / São João bandeirolas flick in a breeze and, in strong wind, the string swings and the flags stream out sideways; the feira tarps' loose hems ripple. All of it is still when calm and only a pixel or so in light wind. In strong, **dry** wind (no gated rain or snow, streets not still wet, a real reading or a labelled test; never with unknown weather) wisps of dust, dry leaves and scraps of paper skitter along the ground from the upwind edge (`src/fx/wind-debris.ts`, at most ~18 particles, fewer when frames get expensive), only in Esperança's outdoor scenes. Painted flags (the Campo da Rodoviária mural) stay still, no props were added for the wind, and indoor scenes (the bus, Partage, the cinema rooms) get no debris. The HUD adds `ventania` to the weather word at the same threshold, and people outdoors in Esperança may comment on it (speech bubbles, above). Preview with `#wx=clear&wind=40`.

**PMPB patrols** (Esperança only; never in Campina Grande, João Pessoa, Areia, Remígio or Tokyo, even though the Paraíba cities share the side-on traffic code): now and then a Polícia Militar da Paraíba patrol car is one of the vehicles in Esperança's street traffic: the side-on roads (`src/actors/br-traffic.ts`: Rua do Comércio, Praça da Cultura, Centro · Casa de Amigos with its one-way road and traffic light, Armazém Paraíba), the one-way road beside the church at Praça da Matriz (`src/actors/matriz-road.ts`) and the depth streets (`src/scenes/persp-kit.ts`: Rua Faustino Porto, Rua José Bezerra da Costa, Rua Manoel Rodrigues de Oliveira). It is only a new vehicle kind (`pmpb`): it enters, queues, stops at the light and yields at the zebra through the same movement code as every other vehicle, so it never drives the wrong way or runs a red. No siren and no new sound: tapping it gives the ordinary car horn. The Capelinha and the Campo turf have no traffic and so no patrols. **Frequency** (`src/actors/police-rules.ts`, tested in `test/police-rules.test.ts`): one cooldown shared by all Esperança street scenes (switching streets neither resets it nor brings a fresh patrol). It runs down only while an Esperança traffic scene is on screen (at most 0.5 s per frame, so a tab back from the background does not burst it); when it reaches zero the next vehicle to enter that road becomes a patrol, and the next wait is drawn from a seeded PRNG: **15–30 min by day, 10–20 min at night** (night = before 06:00 or from 18:30). Opening the app starts a full interval away, and there is at most one patrol on a road. **Livery**, after the PMPB fleet rules (Regulamento de Gestão da Frota da PMPB, Arts. 11–12: light-silver ostensive cars, a reflective black-and-red band edged with white dashes, the prefix on the black and "POLÍCIA MILITAR" on the red; [ptdocz.com](https://ptdocz.com/doc/249226/regulamento-de-gestão-da-frota-da-pmpb)) and a photo of the current Fiat Cronos 2024, viatura 8637 (RBretz, João Pessoa, Jul 2024, on [fotosdeviaturas.net.br](https://www.fotosdeviaturas.net.br/2016/07/viaturas-policiais-brasileiras.html); reference only, not committed): a near-white sedan (the old blue-and-white livery is obsolete), a black rear third with white marks for the prefix and a red band with "PM" in white (the full legend does not fit a 31 px car), white dashes along the band's top, dark windows, silver hubs and a red-and-blue roof bar. The bar is plain paint by day and blinks softly at night (one lamp lit at a time, following the clock, so `#at=` pins it). In the depth views it shows as the black band across the boot (red at the corners) or red on the front wings, with the bar on the roof. **Parked viatura**: on rare 40-minute clock slots (12% of slots by day, 20% at night, from minute 5 to 35; a hash of the slot, so `#at=` reproduces it) a parked PMPB car (lights off, bar dark) and an officer in a plain navy uniform and cap stand on the plaza pavers left of the road at Praça da Matriz, in front of the zebra and clear of the roadway and its yellow curb (asserted in the tests). Tap the officer or the car for a short bubble: "Bom dia, cidadão!" (05–12h), "Boa tarde, cidadão!" (12–18h) or "Boa noite, cidadão!", in turn with "Tudo tranquilo por aqui." and "Qualquer coisa, ligue 190.". Preview with `#police=1` (below).

## Sharing

The address bar always holds the current view: `#city=` and `#scene=` are rewritten (with `history.replaceState`, so no reload and no extra history entries) whenever the city or scene changes, and `#at=` stays there when a moment is pinned. Other options the page was opened with (`#debug`, `#wx=`, `#wind=`, `#car=`, `#police=`, `#egg=`) are left alone in the address bar.

The share button under the eye (top-right, visible even with the HUD hidden) opens two actions:

- **Copiar link**: copies the permalink (city, scene and any pinned `#at=`, never `#debug`, `#wx=`, `#wind=`, `#car=`, `#police=`, `#bus=` or `#egg=`) and shows "Link copiado". A live view gives a live link: whoever opens it sees the same place at its real time and weather.
- **Baixar card**: saves `loficity-<city>-<scene>-<HHMM>.png`, a 1080x1920 story card drawn locally from the scene's framebuffer (rendered once into a portrait frame, through the current palette) with the time, city, scene, date, "LOFI CITY" and the site address.

Nothing is uploaded or posted; the person sends the link or image themselves. The radio and ambience are not touched. Pure rules live in `src/share-rules.ts` (tested in `test/share-rules.test.ts`), the DOM side in `src/share.ts`.

## Keys

The scene changes by itself every 2 minutes (rules in `src/scene-cycle.ts`, unit-tested): the next scene of the city in the list, skipping the bus, the cinema rooms and the mall's inside, which you only ever enter yourself. The timer restarts on any manual scene or city change, never fires while the city map, the place-info dialog or the share menu is open, never pulls you out of the bus, the cinema rooms or the mall, and counts only visible time (a hidden tab does not run it). It starts on, except when the page was opened with `#scene=` or `#cycle=off`; the **Auto** button or A toggles it. The 2 minutes are the owner's choice.

T city map, S scene, A automatic scene change on/off (the **Auto** button), Esc get off the bus, R radio, N next track, P switch station (Lo-fi / Neon), M ambient sound, C CRT, H hide/show the panels, I place info (Esperança scenes), Enter fullscreen. The Konami code (↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ ← → ← → B A) unlocks an easter egg (see below); its final A does not toggle Auto. L triggers a lightning strike only in test mode (`#debug` or a `#wx=` override); in normal use it does nothing and lightning comes only from real storm data.

The city map is a pixel map of Paraíba with a pin per city, generated from the city registry; cities outside Paraíba (Tóquio) are listed under it. Esc, the × button or a tap outside closes it without changing the city.

The eye button (top-right) hides the whole HUD, credit included, for a clean view; the choice is remembered. Hiding it does not pause the radio. The radio tries to start by itself on load; browsers block that until the page has had a gesture, so it then starts on the first tap or key press (unless the listener already used the radio controls). Fullscreen is on the button and Enter only (no double-click), so quick taps on the scene never resize it.

The "i" button under the share button (Esperança scenes only; not rendered elsewhere) opens "Sobre este lugar", a modal with the real story of the current scene's place: title, short paragraphs, curiosities and a list of sources with links. Esc, the × button or a tap outside closes it, and focus goes back to the button; it follows the scene if it changes. The texts live in `src/data/place-info.ts`, one entry per Esperança scene id, using only facts from the cited sources (local history blog História Esperancense, Revivendo Esperança, the Prefeitura, g1, Wikipedia); where no history of a street name or shop was found (Rua Faustino Porto, Rua José Bezerra da Costa, Casa de Amigos) the entry says so and only describes the scene. Tested in `test/place-info.test.ts`.

## Easter eggs

Esperança's scenes hide rare, small homages to geek culture, original pixel figures drawn in code like everything else (no assets, no copied sprites). Rules in `src/actors/egg-rules.ts` (tested in `test/egg-rules.test.ts`), drawing and taps in `src/actors/eggs.ts`, hooked once from `src/main.ts` (update, draw after the scene, tap, hover cursor), so no scene file carries egg code and the other cities never show them.

- **Schedule**: like the little moments, a pure function of the clock: Esperança's local time is cut into 40-minute slots; a hash of (scene, slot) gives a 32% chance of one egg in that slot, picked among those the scene places whose hours fit, lasting 12–45 s, with at least 5 quiet minutes before the next slot. So at most one egg at a time, about one every couple of hours of watching a scene (fewer where the conditions cancel some), and the same `#at=` moment always shows the same thing. `#debug` shows the running egg and the next planned one.
- **Conditions** (hard, can only cancel an egg, never change the weather): night is `S.dayK < 0.2`; rain is `raining()` from `rain-rules.ts` on the gated weather state (or the labelled `#wx=` test); cloudy is `S.cloud >= 0.5` from a genuine reading or the `#wx=` test (`cloudy` gives 0.6, `clear` 0); unknown weather is neither rain nor cloudy.
- **Taps**: tapping an egg while it shows gives a short pt-BR line in a speech bubble (after animals, people and vehicles, before scene props); the mouse cursor turns into a pointer over it.

## Layout

- `src/engine`: indexed framebuffer, palette, WebGL2 post-processing
- `src/sky`: cities, clock and astronomy, live weather (`weather-feed.ts` holds the pure live / stale / unavailable rules and `rain-gate.ts` the pure rain gate, both unit-tested; `live.ts` the network), palette grading; `rhythm.ts` holds the pure, unit-tested daily rhythm (crowds, birds, mass times)
- `src/fx`: rain, snow, petals and leaves, lightning, fireworks
- `src/actors`: pedestrians, cars and motorcycles, trees
- `src/scenes`: shared passes, the Esperança prop kit (`br-kit.ts`) and one file per scene
- `src/data`: the place stories behind the "i" button (`place-info.ts`, pure, unit-tested)
- `src/ui`: the place-info dialog (`place-info.ts`) and the city map (`map-rules.ts` holds the pure, unit-tested projection, label layout and pick/dismiss rules; `paraiba.ts` the state outline)
- `src/audio`: generative ambience, synthesized SFX and church bells (`bells-schedule.ts` holds the pure rules and `bells-scheduler.ts` the timer, both unit-tested), and the radio
- `api/metar.ts`: Vercel edge function, a cacheable same-origin proxy for the METAR (not part of the client build; `pnpm dev` / `pnpm preview` forward the same path in `vite.config.ts`)
- `test`: `node --test` unit tests

## Credits

- **Weather**: [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/) (CC BY 4.0). No API key; refreshes every 10 minutes. Weather is never simulated: if a fetch fails, the last real reading for that city stays on screen marked "dados de HH:MM · sem atualização" (for up to 12 h); with no reading the HUD says "clima indisponível" over a neutral sky with no rain, snow, storm or fog. Failures are retried 3 times (15 s, 1 min, 3 min), then every 10 minutes until the API answers again.
- **Rain gate**: a forecast is a model guess, and in the Agreste the default model often shows drizzle (code 51, ~0.1 mm) on dry days. So rain, drizzle and storm (and everything that follows them: umbrellas, shelters, rain lines, the rain pause of games, fair-weather events, hot-day drinks, thinner crowds, wet streets) appear only when the forecast rate is at least **0.5 mm/h** (Open-Meteo's current value is a 15-minute sum, converted to mm/h), or when the nearby airport **METAR** reports precipitation now: Campina Grande (SBKG) for Esperança, Campina Grande, Remígio and Areia, João Pessoa (SBJP) for João Pessoa; Tokyo uses the threshold only. Conflicts: a forecast at or above the threshold rains even when the METAR is dry (a station 25-40 km away can miss a local cell); below it, a METAR with RA / DZ / SH.. / TS (any `+` / `-` intensity) gives light rain (and a storm only for TS at the station), showers or thunder only in the vicinity (`VCSH`, `VCTS`) give light rain only when the forecast has some precipitation of its own, and a dry, missing, stale (over 90 min) or unparseable METAR gives no rain. Stale Open-Meteo readings go through the same gate; with no reading the sky stays neutral; `#wx=` bypasses the gate. Clouds, temperature, wind, fog and snow come straight from the forecast. Rules in `src/sky/rain-gate.ts`, tests in `test/rain-gate.test.ts`. ## Privacy

The hosted site uses [Vercel Web Analytics](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics) and [Speed Insights](https://vercel.com/docs/speed-insights). Both are cookieless and only collect visit counts per city (`/tokyo`, `/esperanca`) and performance metrics. Nothing is sent when running locally.
